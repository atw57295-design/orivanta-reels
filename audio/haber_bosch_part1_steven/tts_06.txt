On September 9th, 1913, the world's first ammonia synthesis plant went into operation at Oppau — today part of Ludwigshafen, on the Rhine.

According to BASF, the new plant could produce up to... thirty tonnes of ammonia a day.

Nobody yet knew exactly how farmers' crops would respond to this new fertilizer. So in the spring of 1914, on Bosch's proposal, BASF set up an agricultural research station at Limburgerhof, near Ludwigshafen, to find out.

What started as drops on a tabletop became one of the most important industrial processes ever created. Synthetic fertilizer spread across the world through the twentieth century, helping farmers grow far more food on the same land.

Researchers estimate that by the early twenty-first century, close to... half of the world's population was being fed by food grown with nitrogen from this process.

Crookes's famine never came the way he feared.

The chemists had pulled bread out of the air.
