The process would be called the Haber process. Later, the Haber-Bosch process.

But look closely... at the paperwork.

On the American patent, Haber put Le Rossignol's name beside his own — and gave him a share of the royalties that would one day make him rich.

Le Rossignol later said that Haber had handled the whole patent matter. He left Karlsruhe in August 1909, just as BASF began the long work of industrialization.

He went on to a career in industrial research in Britain, and used his royalty income for philanthropy.

The Nobel Prize, when it came, went to Haber alone. Le Rossignol's name faded from the story.
