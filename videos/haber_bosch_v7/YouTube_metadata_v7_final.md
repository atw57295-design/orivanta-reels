TITLE
Bread From Air: The Forgotten Man Behind the Machine That Feeds Half the World

DESCRIPTION
By some estimates, half the nitrogen in your body was pulled from the air by a machine. This is the story of how three men cracked one of chemistry's hardest problems: Fritz Haber, Robert Le Rossignol and Carl Bosch. Only two became famous. And the same chemistry that fed billions would also fuel a war.

In 1898, Sir William Crookes warned that the world was running out of nitrogen for its wheat. Chile, Peru and Bolivia had already fought a war over desert nitrate. Then, on July 2, 1909, a tabletop machine in Karlsruhe dripped liquid ammonia made from air, and the world changed.

Who deserves the credit for an invention: the person with the idea, the person who builds the first working machine, or the person who makes it work for millions? Tell us in the comments.
