## Description
By some estimates, half the nitrogen in your body was pulled from the air by a machine.

In 1898, Sir William Crookes warned that the world was heading for a wheat shortage. Nitrogen fertilizer came from seabird guano and Chilean nitrate, and those deposits had already started a war. Then Fritz Haber, his British partner Robert Le Rossignol, and BASF engineer Carl Bosch found a way to make ammonia straight from the air.

This is the story of the tabletop machine, the name on the patent that history forgot, the steel tubes that burst, and the Oppau plant that changed how the world eats.

Who deserves the credit for an invention: the idea, the first working machine, or the scale? Tell us in the comments.
